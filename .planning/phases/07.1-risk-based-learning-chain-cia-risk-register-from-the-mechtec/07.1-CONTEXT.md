# Phase 07.1: Risk-Based Learning Chain - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-11-12
**Status:** Ready for planning

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Make the chain *company profile → risks (CIA, likelihood, business impact) → measures → budget* explicit for teams, so that risk-based decisions are practised rather than discovered by chance. Deliverables are print material and flow changes only:

- a risk register with a 3×3 risk matrix as the new first Teamheft page;
- a "gegen R#" link from measures to risks;
- a reality-check line on each attack worksheet;
- a closing "Rückblick" in the register;
- RoS framed as damage avoided per euro;
- a debriefing rebuilt around the chain, with an optional management pitch;
- the schedule, intro deck and facilitator booklet adapted to all of the above.

No game formula and no balance value changes. The phase ships as print release candidate 3, printed and approved by the user, **before the dry run on 2026-11-25** (Phase 7 plan 07-12, which then measures the new flow).

Background: the user's learning-goal priorities (2026-11-12):
1. **Core:** risks are worked out from the company and measures are set on that basis, using the CIA triad.
2. **Keep:** budget prioritisation.
3. **Business impact:** married to the risk assessment.
4. **Secondary:** stakeholder communication.

A pedagogical review found the threat model only implicit: the clues sit in the story documents and the debriefing asks about them only afterwards.

</domain>

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Risk register (new first Teamheft page)
- **D-01:** The register has **5 rows (R1–R5)**. That is enough to cover all later attacks while still forcing a choice.
- **D-02:** Columns per risk:
  - asset ("what is valuable?");
  - threat or weakness, with the source document;
  - protection goal **C / I / A** (the core of the CIA learning goal);
  - business-impact **category tick**, plus **one sentence**. Candidate categories: production stop, main customer/OEM, know-how, costs; final wording is at Claude's discretion;
  - **rank 1–5**;
  - **treat / accept** tick, plus one line of reasoning;
  - **Rückblick** after wave 3: correctly rated, underestimated or overestimated.
- **D-03:** Likelihood × impact is assessed in a **3×3 risk matrix** printed on the same page, which the user found illustrative. Teams write the risk numbers (R1–R5) into the cells.
  - The matrix has **colour zones** (red, yellow, green).
  - The **rank column** in the table gives an unambiguous order for budget prioritisation.
- **D-04:** The matrix's impact axis is labelled with **€ bands for low, medium and high**.
  - The bands are numbers, so they live in `simulation_config.json` (new block) and never in prose.
  - They must be realistic relative to the game's damage values: an unprotected attack costs 140,000–320,000 €, and the worst case is 620,000 € in total.
  - The planner proposes the exact limits from the game values. A starting point: low < 50,000 €, medium 50,000–150,000 €, high > 150,000 €.
  - The validator checks the bands, ascending and non-overlapping.
- **D-05:** Teams may **ask the facilitator what a damage would mean for the business**. This is the user's chosen communication practice: secondary goal, but welcome.
  - The facilitator answers with a **€ order of magnitude** from the bands, in the role of MechTech's management.
- **D-06:** The facilitator booklet gets a **cheat sheet of 6–8 typical business cases**, each with an answer and a band.
  - Examples: "production stops for a day", "design data ends up with a competitor", "the OEM puts orders on hold".
  - Cases are phrased as **business consequences, never as attacks**, so nothing is revealed. The figures come from config, the prose from `texte.toml`.
- **D-07:** The register page carries a short instruction and **one filled-in example risk from a different industry**, so the example reveals nothing about MechTech.
- **D-08:** The Teamheft becomes **5 pages: register, planning, tracking, RoS, Budget Review**.
  - Corrected at the plan-phase review (2026-11-12): the RoS part is already two pages (RoS and Budget Review), so the register makes five pages, not four.
  - The Teamheft is therefore **handed out in the story block** (previously in "Maßnahmen + Budget").
  - The schedule directions and the print list must follow this.

### Measure ↔ risk
- **D-09:** The existing planning table on Teamheft page 2 gets a new column **"gegen R#"**. Teams enter one or more risks per measure, for example "R1, R3".
- **D-10:** Filling the column is a **recommendation, not mandatory**. There is no penalty, no rule change and no facilitator check.
- **D-11:** **Risk acceptance is explicit:** the register's treat/accept tick plus a line of reasoning (D-02). It feeds the debriefing question "Welches akzeptierte Risiko hat euch getroffen?"

### Reality check per wave
- **D-12:** Each attack worksheet ends with **one line**: which of your risks did this attack hit, entered as **R# or "nicht im Register"**.
  - The worksheet is handed out only when its wave starts, so this reveals nothing in advance.
  - Wording must pass the veil and giveaway checks.
- **D-13:** **No re-ranking in the change window.**
  - The user pointed out that re-prioritising is limited by the tight budget.
  - Instead, the register's **Rückblick column** is filled after wave 3.
  - Downgrade and cancel stay the existing means of moving budget between risks; no new rule.

### Time, debriefing, RoS and introduction
- **D-14:** The story block grows from **25 to 35 minutes**, because reading, collecting clues and filling in the register don't fit in 25.
- **D-15:** The debriefing grows from **20 to 30 minutes**. Stages: reaction 3, description 5, **analysis 14**, application 8.
  - The analysis questions are built around the chain:
    - register vs. reality;
    - accepted risks that hit;
    - the team's C/I/A assessment vs. the wave weightings;
    - which risks the money paid off against (RoS).
  - The application questions transfer to real risk management, for example building a risk register for a real client.
- **D-16:** The session total becomes **215 minutes** (195 + 10 + 10).
  - The booked slot on 2026-11-25 is 13:00–17:00 (240 min), which leaves 25 minutes of buffer.
  - Wave minutes are unchanged (D-34 of Phase 7: wave 1 = 25).
  - The schedule tests and config are updated test-first. This supersedes Phase 7 D-22 and D-34 only for the block durations named here.
- **D-17:** The **optional 60-second management pitch** per team ("Warum war unser Budget gut investiert?") goes into the booklet as an "if time allows" step.
  - It has **no planned minutes**; the facilitator decides on the spot.
- **D-18:** **RoS framing**: an explanatory sentence on the Teamheft RoS page and one analysis question in the debriefing, both framing RoS as **net damage avoided per invested euro**: how much damage each invested euro prevented beyond its own cost; above zero means security prevented more damage than it cost.
  - Corrected at the plan-phase review (user choice 2026-11-12, "Netto-Formulierung"): the original phrase "verhinderter Schaden pro investiertem Euro" equals RoS + 1, because `rules.ros` computes (avoided damage − own cost) / own cost, with avoided damage = `rules.base_losses` − total damage. The wording must match that formula.
  - The formula and the printed calculation stay unchanged.
- **D-19:** **Intro deck**: a **new slide "Risiko-Matrix"** in the introduction, explaining likelihood × impact, the zones and how the register is used, with no MechTech or attack content. The minute figures on the affected slides are updated too: story block and debriefing.
  - Same procedure as Phase 7 D-18: python-pptx, run-text edits where possible, a checklist approved by the user before the edit, user review of the result.
  - The deck binary is committed once.
  - The intro block stays 10 minutes.

### Plan-phase review (2026-11-12)
- **D-20:** **Rückblick reference** (user choice "Voller Schaden"): teams judge each register risk on two things.
  - Likelihood: was the risk hit by an attack (the reality-check lines of D-12)?
  - Impact: the attack's **full damage without measures** (case 1 on its worksheet) against the team's € band.
  - A risk the team protected well therefore still counts as "richtig eingeschätzt"; the residual damage after measures is not the reference.
  - How the instruction treats a risk that no attack hit is wording at Claude's discretion.
- **D-21:** **Rückblick timing** (user choice "Abschluss-Block"): teams fill in the Rückblick column in the closing block, together with the Budget Review and the RoS page. The closing block keeps its 10 minutes; its direction text names the Rückblick. The debriefing analysis then starts from a completed register.
- **D-22:** **Cheat-sheet consistency**: the € bands of D-04 and the band of every cheat-sheet case (D-06) must agree with the game's damage values, so a band a team hears in the story block is the band the attack worksheet later confirms.
  - Unprotected damage per attack is `base_severity` × `s_unit` (thousand €): ransomware 160,000 €, OT 320,000 €, exfil 140,000 €; the sum, 620,000 €, is `rules.base_losses`, the RoS reference.
- **D-23:** **Requirements**: the phase covers RISK-01..RISK-10 in `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md`, one plan per requirement (user approval 2026-11-12). Each plan also updates, for its own part, the `README.md` rulebook (CLAUDE.md change order: config → README → texte.toml) and the "Was ist neu" sheet (`sheets.was_neu.*`) where the sheet describes what the plan changes.

### Claude's Discretion
- Exact German wording of every new text (instructions, column headers, categories, cheat-sheet cases, debriefing questions). Texts must follow the existing style and pass the digit and giveaway lints.
- The layout of the register page, including whether matrix and table sit side by side or stacked, as long as it fits one A4 page and passes the fill report.
- The exact € band limits, proposed by the planner from the game values (D-04).
- How the plans split the work, one requirement per plan per the user's global rule. The requirement IDs are now fixed (D-23).
- How `tests/test_intro_deck.py` stays honest between the schedule plan (RISK-08) and the deck plan (RISK-09). The test composes the slide minute figures from `session_schedule`, so the schedule change turns it red until the single deck edit lands. A strict xfail bridge (precedent: Phase 6 TUNING_TARGET / 06-10 re-pin) or an order that edits config and deck back to back are both acceptable.

<specifics>
## Specific Ideas

- The user wants the matrix because it is illustrative ("schön anschaulich").
- Business impact doubles as a communication exercise: teams ask "what would the damage be if … happened?" and the facilitator answers with a € order of magnitude. The user explicitly asked for definitions of low and high damage.
- The example risk on the register page comes from a different industry, so it teaches the method without hinting at MechTech's weak points.
